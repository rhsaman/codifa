# brainstorming

Use when the user needs ideas: names, concepts, content angles, solutions, or creative directions.

## Process

1. **Set the goal.** What is the idea for? Write a clear prompt (e.g., "a name for a productivity app for designers"). Constraints: audience, tone, length, no-go areas.
2. **Diverge first — quantity over quality.** Generate many ideas without judging. Aim for variety: literal, playful, abstract, opposite, absurd. Wild ideas often spark usable ones.
3. **Mix and mutate.** Combine elements of different ideas. Steal-and-improve from adjacent fields. Reverse assumptions ("what if it worked the opposite way?").
4. **Converge.** Group ideas into themes. Mark the strongest 3-5 by fit with the goal, not just preference.
5. **Refine the shortlist.** For each top idea: one-line pitch, why it works, possible objections.
6. **Recommend.** Name the best option with reasoning, and offer 2-3 alternatives. Ask which direction to develop further.

## Techniques to try

- **SCAMPER**: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse.
- **Analogies**: what does an expert in another field do here?
- **Constraint shifts**: force a small budget, a short deadline, or a tiny audience.
